## Resolved <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G00A' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a>

- **The document is flat where the subject matter is layered**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G003' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> all eighteen items sit at one rank, so a statement the whole skill depends on ("work the graph, not a folder of files") is typographically indistinguishable from a shell-quoting gotcha. A reader has no way to tell which items explain the others and which are implementation detail, and no basis for deciding what to re-read when something goes wrong. The target shape is the four levels in [Architecture](../design/aperas-skill.md#id/BlockNode:00CEA5Y5JG003). **Resolved**: four levels now, Philosophy through Mechanics.
- **The load-bearing statement is last**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G004' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the graph-substance principle is item 18, reached only after seventeen mechanical rules that are themselves consequences of it. A reader who stops early gets the consequences without the cause — exactly the failure mode that produced the grep-instead-of-traverse incident, where the rule was cited correctly in a report while being violated in the same breath. **Resolved**: the graph-substance principle is promoted to Orientation, ahead of every mechanical rule that follows from it.
- **Settled facts and live cautions are not distinguished**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G005' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> item 13's create-and-promote dance describes a failure mode a completed migration has since removed, but nothing at the item says so, so a reader keeps paying a cost whose cause is gone. There is no marking that separates "confirmed safe now" from "still open" from "never reproduced". **Resolved**: every item is marked `[current]`, `[superseded]` or `[unverified]` where it stands.
- **The two section headings don't carve at a real joint**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G006' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> "Editing discipline" (items 1-8) and "Working practices" (items 9-18) split what is actually a mix — wikilink syntax and anchor placement are mechanics, staging and write-discussion-first are discipline, and the graph-substance principle is neither. The boundary reflects the order things were learned, not any distinction a reader can use. **Resolved**: both headings are gone, replaced by the level split.
- **Rule and origin are inconsistently ordered within items**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G007' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> some items lead with the rule and attach the incident; others open with narrative and leave the rule to be inferred. The incident must travel with the rule — a rule whose "why" is lost gets misapplied at its edges — but it should not have to be excavated. **Resolved**: items are stated rule-first, with the incident attached rather than leading.
- **No stated scope boundary**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CEA5KW4G008' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> nothing says what belongs in this skill versus `kg-doc-ingest`, versus a concern doc in the graph, versus a tool-gap issue. Tool gaps discovered in practice have accumulated inline as prose caveats (items 8, 17) rather than being tracked where they can be closed. **Resolved**: stated in the skill — this skill owns working the graph, `kg-doc-ingest` owns getting existing text into it, and project facts live in concern docs the skill only points at.

- **The skill states the citation-direction constraint but never the obligation that follows from it**: because an `issues` block may not cite a `discussion` block, a finding promoted up out of discussion can only be linked from its origin — the discussion entry has to gain the forward link at the moment of promotion. Nothing in the skill says so. Citation direction states what is forbidden and stops; *Discussion is where meta-info is born* argues the opposite caution, against promoting prematurely, which primes hesitation before filing rather than care once filing is legitimate; *Dense linking is the precondition* states the principle with no trigger attached to it. The passage that would name the missing step — [Crystallization — content and topology](../design/aperas-skill.md#id/BlockNode:00CEA5Y5JG00J), whose topology axis is exactly this lifecycle — exists only in the design doc and was never carried into the operational skill file by the v1 restructure. Confirmed live: seven issues filed into a new concern from findings recorded across four discussion docs, every origin left unlinked, invisible from the new doc's own side and caught only by direct user callout. **Resolved**: the skill gained a `### Promotion links at the origin` section under Discipline, directly after Citation direction — stating that the link is added at the origin pointing up, that it happens at the moment of promotion rather than as a later tidying pass, and naming crystallization's topology axis with a pointer to the design doc rather than importing it wholesale.

- **Nothing in the skill fires on a read**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CFA7PQ1R001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> orientation is stated only as step 1 of the edit loop, so a task whose whole investigation phase is reading concerns never enters the loop and never reaches a rule at all. Confirmed live: a session that loaded the skill on turn one and consented to it still read the entire task context out of `artifacts/**.md` with `Read`/`find`/`grep`, and made its first `aperas` call only when a plan step mechanically required the CLI — after the code edit had already landed. The fix is a mechanical substitution rule at Orientation: an `artifacts/**.md` path about to become an argument to `Read`/`grep`/`find`/`cat` is a node reference, and `aperas unfold <ref>` is what it meant. **Resolved in v1.4**: Orientation now carries the substitution rule — an `artifacts/**.md` path bound for `Read`/`grep`/`find`/`cat` is a node reference, and `aperas unfold` is what the call meant — stated for reading rather than only for writing, and naming `Apeiron/*.jsonld` and source code as the two things deliberately not over the line.
- **"The edit loop" is a misnomer that causes the failure it should prevent**: the name tells a reader the section applies once they start writing, so a long read-only phase — and any source-code work — reads as outside it. It is the workflow itself, not an edit procedure: the same opening applies whether the task ends in a traversal, a graph change, or a code change, and only the tail differs. Renaming it and stating its entry condition is what makes the read-side and code-side rules reachable instead of bolted on. **Resolved in v1.4**: the section is now *The loop*, with its entry condition stated — reading a concern, touching source the graph covers, or changing the graph all put you in it — one shared opening and a branching tail.
- **The shadow rule's only worked example is structural, so reading a projection for content reads as exempt**: Philosophy contrasts a `grep` over projections against one `aperas backlinks` call — a question about link structure. Nothing states the content case, and for pure text retrieval the projection genuinely is equivalent, so an agent asking content questions correctly concludes the example doesn't apply to it. The missing corollary is already implied by Philosophy's own sentence that a node's meaning is constituted by what it links to, what links back, and where it sits: a projection carries the text and drops precisely what constitutes the meaning. **Resolved in v1.4**: Philosophy now states the content case directly, that a projection carries a block's words and none of its place, so the loss is invisible precisely because the words are identical.
- **No entry point for source-code work the graph already carries a concern about, at either end**: a task framed as "fix this bug in `reconcile.ts`" matches nothing in the skill, though the graph very likely holds issues, design, planning and history for that exact file. Two obligations are missing rather than one — before the work, find what the graph already knows (a root cause identified, a fix already ordered, an approach already rejected); after it, record what the work found, since a fix landed with nothing written is invisible from every side that can be consulted. **Resolved in v1.4**: Discipline gains *Source the graph already has a concern about*, obliging a lookup before the source is read and a recording after the work lands, with the loop's second trigger pointing at it.
- **`scripts/show_node.py` is documented without noting it reads the on-disk mirror**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00CFA7PQ20003' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> Mechanics presents it as the reader for raw node state, but it parses `Apeiron/*.jsonld` directly, so after an unflushed mutation it reports pre-flush state while `aperas unfold`/`tree` report the live service's. Confirmed live: a move and a tombstone were both invisible to it, producing a confident and wrong conclusion that the move had gone backwards — recovered only by flushing and re-reading. **Resolved in v1.4**: Mechanics now says the script reads the on-disk mirror and reports pre-flush state after an unflushed mutation, where `aperas unfold`/`tree` report the live service's.

- **The identity-preservation rule is keyed to a verb, so a parent-heading `update` slips past it**: "Move a node; don't remove and recreate it" names `remove` + `insert` as the anti-pattern, but `aperas update` on a parent heading recreates a whole subtree just as thoroughly, with the tombstoning arriving as a side effect of reconciliation rather than as anything the operator typed. Confirmed live: regrouping a flat 17-item Task Breakdown into three sub-headings came back `0 matched, 20 added, 17 removed`. The rule needs restating outcome-first — any operation that can tombstone a node meant to be kept — so it catches whatever verb produces that outcome instead of the one verb that was noticed first. **Resolved in v1.4**: the rule is now *Preserve identity — against any operation that can tombstone a node you meant to keep*, enumerating all three routes (`remove`+`insert`, a parent-heading `update`, and a depth-changing full-list push) rather than the one that looks like a delete, and adding that zero backlinks is not a licence.
- **Mechanics promises id preservation with no precondition, and the precondition is the whole story**: "Adding an item to an existing list" tells the reader to target the parent heading and states that exact-key matching reuses every unchanged item's id, calling it the only safe route for an ordered list. That promise holds only while a node keeps its parent and its depth; a push that moves items to a new depth silently matches nothing. Nothing in the section says so, so two successful appends read as licence to attempt a restructure the same way. **Resolved in v1.4**: the promise now carries its precondition — it holds only while every item keeps its parent and its depth — and says outright that stating it unconditionally is how it got read as licence for a restructure.
- **Discipline and Mechanics give opposite instructions for the same task, and nothing ranks them**: for relocating items inside an ordered list, Discipline says move the node while Mechanics says the parent-heading push is the only safe route. Mechanics is more specific and more operational, so it wins by default in exactly the case where it is wrong. Neither section acknowledges the other; whichever fix lands should leave a pointer in both directions rather than assuming a reader arrives at the right one. **Resolved in v1.4**: the two sections now point at each other, *Preserve identity* naming the matching promise it constrains and *Adding an item to an existing list* naming moves as the route for a depth change.

- **The provenance obligation is scoped to promotion, so a claim added to an entry already in place carries no source**: *Promotion links at the origin* describes the failure exactly — a formal entry paraphrasing a confirmed finding without linking it, complete-looking from its own side, with `aperas backlinks` returning nothing — but scopes itself to the moment a finding crystallizes up out of `discussion`. An edit that adds supporting evidence to an entry already in the doc is not a promotion, and nothing else in the skill covers it. Confirmed live: `planning/aperas-skill.md`'s trigger-accuracy item was edited to cite three recorded observations and came out with `links: 0`, caught only when a reader ran `backlinks` on it. The same uncovered gap let through two claims that were wrong in kind rather than merely unsourced — a version label inferred and written as recorded, and a task shape quoted as though it were a labelled prompt. Note this is ["The skill states the citation-direction constraint but never the obligation that follows from it"](#id/BlockNode:00CEA5Y5JG00J) resurfacing one case over, since that fix was scoped to the incident which produced it; and that the underlying notion is older than either — `archive/Aperas-design.md` defines deep read as provenance projection, a definition `SKILL.md` did not carry across. **Resolved in v1.4**: the rule is now *Every claim carries its source*, stated as a property of the claim rather than a step in the promotion ritual, requiring `aperas backlinks` to be run on what was just written, and grounded in the new Philosophy statement that a claim and its provenance are one object.

- **The inline status vocabulary duplicates the snapshot thread and taxes every item to mark an exception that never arrived**: *How to read this* required each item to be marked `[current]`, `[superseded]` or `[unverified]`, on the reasoning that a caution whose cause has been removed is worse than no caution. That reasoning holds; the instrument does not. This concern's own snapshot-and-delta thread already records the same distinction more precisely, per item and per version, without touching the rules themselves — so the tag was a second, coarser copy of an existing record. And across v1 through v1.4 nothing was ever marked `[unverified]` and only one thing `[superseded]`, so 36 of 38 items carried a marker whose only content was that there was nothing to say. **Resolved in v2**: the vocabulary is gone and the rule is narrower — only current, verified items appear in the file at all, with superseded material and unverified hypotheses staying in `discussion/aperas-skill.md`. Recorded in [Topology](../design/aperas-skill.md#id/BlockNode:00CEA5Y5JG00C).
- **Philosophy states the shadow rule for writing only, so the read side arrives as a correction four items later**: the founding statement ran "a projection can be regenerated at any time; it is never the place a change is made" — write-side throughout, with editing named as the error. Reading and searching the shadow are the same category error, and the file said so only afterwards, in a paragraph beginning "And the content case is not the safe half" that existed to correct the paragraph above it rather than to state anything of its own. A reader who stops before reaching it takes away a rule about writing. This is the read-side gap of [the invocation-is-not-governance issue](#id/BlockNode:00CFA7PQ1R001) at the level that explains it: that one fixed where orientation fires, this one fixes what the principle underneath it actually says. **Resolved in v2**: the shadow rule now names both — never where a change is made, never where a question is answered — and spells out the three failures (a write that leaves the source untouched, a read that drops the block's place, a structural question the projection cannot answer at all), which lets the corrective paragraph be absorbed rather than kept.
- **Traversal is described in two directions and the missing one is the cheapest**: *Deep read runs in two directions* named forward/downward for content and backward for context, and left out upward entirely. Ancestors are context, and climbing one or two levels is precisely what an agent is reaching for when it opens a whole artifact instead — the most common way a read leaves the graph for the shadow, and the one the file gave no cheaper alternative to. For a node at `discussion/a.md/h1/freeflow/a/aa/aaa`, unfolding `discussion/a.md/h1/freeflow/a` is usually already enough, and the climb can stop as soon as the block's meaning stops changing. The section also governed deep write while naming only deep read in its title. **Resolved in v2**: retitled to cover both operations, with upward added as its own direction and a stopping rule attached, and deep write folded in as the backward-inherent case rather than left as a trailing paragraph.
- **Five items narrate the document's own past instead of stating the rule, and the cause is the context skill the document itself teaches**: *The loop* opened by disclaiming its former name, the provenance bullet explained which half of backlinks "was missing", *Citation direction* closed on how the rule had been generalized, the list-matching promise recalled how it used to be worded, and *Every claim carries its source* explained what a promotion-scoped version of itself would fail to reach. Each was a correction bolted onto text left unfixed. The phrases were accurate in the plan they were argued in, where the surrounding discussion supplied their referent; in `SKILL.md` the only context is the block and the blocks above it, and there they read as scars from a conversation the reader never saw. That is the same upward-context check the entry above adds for reading, failed on the writing side. **Resolved in v2**: all five originals rewritten to stand alone, and the general rule recorded in [Topology](../design/aperas-skill.md#id/BlockNode:00CGF54B5G001) — the document's own past belongs in the graph, provenance stays, and the test is whether a sentence still reads to someone with no memory of the version before it.
