# Core — History <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z0001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a>

## Current Status <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z0002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a>

As of 2026-10-12 two commits of store performance work have landed on branch `oxigraph`, and both branches (`oxigraph` and `js-apeironngn`) point at the second one, `d794060`.

- The removal that stalled the service for minutes (a full-store scan for every embedded node removed) is replaced by one object-bound match; one removal in the live service of a task of 164 blocks and 101 embedded `Link` nodes took 3.7 s.
- The `free()` patch is committed as `d794060` on branch `oxigraph`: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the reads and writes of the Oxigraph store go through one helper that frees every wasm object at the root; in a scratch process on a copy of the mirror, eight scans of all ids by kind stay flat in memory, and the dehydrate of both mirrors falls from 33 to 38 s to 2.5 to 3.8 s. A fresh service answered 17 s after its process started at 379 MB resident, where a fresh service had idled at 1.05 to 1.5 GB (one sample).
- Not shown: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the trend of resident memory over a long run (it still rose with writes in the two short runs recorded, with no plateau seen), the gain split by site, and whether releasing the old `Store` at reload is a gain or a hazard.
- The memory issue and the full-store-scans issue stay open in [the core issues](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00CVQ4Z968001); [the scans issue](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D0W02KPG001) holds the candidate layers (bound matches, an index, a record cache and the pause), which are parked and not tried.
- Oxigraph patching is paused. The plain JavaScript engine is begun on `js-apeironngn`: the contract the code asks of the store is surveyed, [the cost of a write](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D22Z1HJ8001) is profiled, the engine is designed and planned in [Core — Planning](../planning/core.md#id/BlockNode:00CF23FB1G001), and [Batch 15](../planning/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D4NBFKAG001) moved the current code to `core/src/shell/` on 2026-10-16 (staged, not committed), so that `core/src/apeironNgn/` is free for the engine; no engine code is on the branch yet. It is to be compared with the `free()` version on the same mirror; no comparison has been measured.
- The design of the engine is drafted in [Core — Design](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D24N87SR001), with the user from 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-15, and fact-checked against the code and the mirror on 2026-10-12; the work that remains is listed under its [Open points](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D24N87T000J), and the service lifecycle stays outside it.

## Milestones <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8005' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a>

- Until 2026-09-02, TerminusDB, the first substrate: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV228001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the graph lived in a TerminusDB database that the JSON-LD mirror in git reflected. The reasons and the design are recorded in the archived `Aperas-design.md` ("Stack Selection Rationales" and "Phase 0.1: Substrate Evolution"); the TerminusDB designs themselves are under `tdb-era/` in the archive.
  
  - **Why it was chosen**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV228002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> immutable delta layers with Git-like branches, time travel, diffs and merge requests; JSON-LD document classes that map onto block trees and nested subdocuments; WOQL and generated GraphQL as the two query surfaces.
  - **What kept the move cheap**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G000' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the mirror already round-tripped as plain JSON-LD, so "nothing about the substrate's actual content depends on TerminusDB being the engine", and every TerminusDB call sat behind named modules (`client.ts`, `crud.ts`, `woql.ts`, `graphql.ts`).
  - **Superseded by**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> ApeironNgn (next entry). The roadmap named the successor "a minimal Rust graph engine scoped to exactly what Aperas exercises".
- 2026-09-02, ApeironNgn designed, and the reasons for leaving TerminusDB: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> from the commits of the archived `Aperas-apeironngn-design.md` (sections 1 and 2); the findings were read from that file and not re-run.
  
  - **Dates**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G003' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the archived file does not date its steps; the dates in these entries are those of the commits that changed it, from 2026-09-02 to 2026-09-07, and a step's date is the range in which it landed.
  - **The stated reason**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G004' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> architectural fit, "not a response to a measured bottleneck" (a bulk tree fetch took 18 ms for 91 nodes). TerminusDB made the projection happen twice, once into its own JSON-LD schema and again into the differently shaped results of WOQL, GraphQL and the Document API; ApeironNgn's promise was one projection, in which the class is the schema and the traversal.
  - **What was found against the live TerminusDB**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G005' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `List` fields give plain `triple()` no shortcut in either direction (no reverse lookup, and the forward lookup binds to the internal `Cons` cell and needs an `rdf:first` and `rdf:rest` walk); `@abstract` classes do not become GraphQL interfaces; rebuilding a tree node by node lost to the bulk fetch by 47 to 240 times because no API can ask for "whatever fields this type has" without knowing the type; two upstream bugs filed, `terminusdb/terminusdb#2509` (bundle and unbundle across stores) and `terminusdb/terminusdb#2512` (`@unfold` on a `List` field); a server panic from a string truncated inside a multi-byte UTF-8 character, reproduced twice.
- 2026-09-02, ApeironNgn on Oxigraph, in memory: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G006' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the design and its first build, from the archived design (section 3 and section 4, step 1), checked then node for node against the raw JSON-LD of the real mirror.
  
  - **The engine**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G007' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> Oxigraph, an off-the-shelf embeddable RDF store with SPO, POS and OSP indexes, used synchronously from Node, chosen over a build from scratch.
  - **Containment**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G008' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> reified `parent` and `siblingIndex` triples instead of `rdf:List`, so both directions are cheap and the order comes from sorting a fetched set.
  - **The schema is the class**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G009' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a per-class table of each field's cardinality (`one`, `optional`, `set`, `orderedContainment`) and storage kind (`reference`, `embed`), written against the classes and deliberately not derived from TerminusDB's `schema.json`, which had already drifted from the live schema. The first reading interface was a `Proxy` for each node, so that `a.b.c` was a literal property access.
  - **Storage and scope**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00A' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> in memory only, because the Node and WASM build has no on-disk store; "revisit only once the KG's actual size makes in-memory rehydration or working-set size a real problem (GB-level)". Versioning is git on the mirror; concurrency is not supported by design, and search needs a secondary index under any engine.
  - **Behaviour it gave without being asked**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3F6J4JG001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> it refused an id that was not a valid IRI, and it rewrote a non-canonical integer into its canonical form. Whether the engine keeps either is an Open point of [the design](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D24N87T000S).
  - **Superseded by**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00B' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the `Proxy` (step 3, below), the hierarchy it served, and the revisit trigger, which was met by the overhead of the wrapper objects and not by the size of the data (see [the scans issue](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D0W02KPG001)).
- 2026-09-02 to 2026-09-03, the scripts migrated one at a time: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00C' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> each command was given an ApeironNgn implementation, run on the same real data and diffed against its TerminusDB output before the next (archived design, section 4, step 2).
  
  - **Read paths**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00D' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `kg:tree`, `kg:path`, `kg:resolve` (both tiers) and `kg:project` were diffed clean, including the error paths, once the diff harness compared standard error and exit status as well as standard output. `kg:project` was clean on 17 of 18 artifacts; the 18th failed on the TerminusDB side with a server error at that document's size.
  - **Write paths**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00E' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> from `kg:unfold` on, a write was checked on a scratch copy of the mirror and not by a live diff, because a write-capable script run on the real paths changes the real mirror on success. `kg:title`, `kg:link`, `kg:track` and `kg:ingest` were verified that way.
  - **The write-back**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00F' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `dehydrateToJsonLd` rewrote the real mirror to a scratch directory with zero semantic mismatches over all of its documents. One real bug: Oxigraph canonicalised an `xsd:dateTime` literal on read-back and rewrote `...27.480Z` to `...27.48Z`; date-like strings are no longer typed as dates.
  - **Not migrated**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00G' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `kg:export` and `kg:import` were retired, since they only bridged TerminusDB and the mirror; `kg:search` waited for a secondary index; `Assertion` and `kg:assert` were left out.
- 2026-09-03, step 3: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00H' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the `Proxy` replaced by classes, and the hierarchy made real: from the archived design (step 3).
  
  - **Accessors**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00J' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> each leaf class got real `get` and `set` accessors on a sealed instance, so an unknown read gives `undefined` and an unknown write throws, with the error a generic `TypeError`.
  - **Hierarchy**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00K' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the flat classes became `ApeironInstance`, `BaseNode`, `TreeNode` and `BlockNode`, `ArtifactNode`, `FolderNode`; `Link` and `StringProp` became leaf subdocuments.
  - **The key**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00M' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `blockId`, `artifactId` and `folderId` were replaced by one derived `key` (the part of the id after its kind), so the mirror stopped writing them, and the old `kg:import` could no longer read it, which was accepted.
  - **`Link` as an embedded node**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00N' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> it shed the base node fields and kept `target` and `predicate`, and the hand-made "mint an id, then attach" steps went away. `Assertion` and `BaseEdge` were not migrated in any form: a claim linking two nodes is a block of prose with a wikilink, and the real `Assertion` file was empty.
  - **Functions folded into methods by a rule**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00P' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a function folds onto a class when one identified node is a natural `this`; a store-wide search or sweep stays free.
  - **Bugs found and fixed**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00Q' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> an embed carry-forward that destroyed a link by treating a bare id string as an object; `props` and `frontmatter` receiving new ids on every ingest (now matched by key and value); the order of a set field not stable across a rehydrate and dehydrate; a test of liveness written as `tombstonedAt !== true`, which is true for every node (the field holds a date string); the projection lacking a final newline.
- 2026-09-03, step 4: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00R' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the real corpus validated and the TerminusDB code archived: from the archived design (step 4).
  
  - **First run on the real corpus**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00S' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> 13 artifacts with no real change came back byte-identical, which the TerminusDB path did not achieve on the same corpus; the 5 that changed reconciled sanely.
  - **The tests**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00T' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `verifyPhase0.ts` was replaced by a suite that runs on an in-memory store and never writes back to the real mirror. On its first run it found that a freshly minted `Link.target` was stored as a literal and not as a reference.
  - **The hooks and the archive**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00V' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the git hooks were moved from the TerminusDB `kg:track` to the ApeironNgn one, and the TerminusDB scripts were renamed with a `Tdb` suffix and archived under `web/.archive`, with the shared modules they need copied beside them as a frozen snapshot.
- 2026-09-03, step 5: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00W' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the shared service process: from the archived design (step 5); the mechanics of the service remain recorded only there, since no design document describes the service yet.
  
  - **What replaced what**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00X' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> every command used to start a process that rehydrated and dehydrated the whole mirror; one long-running service now holds one store, and every command is a thin client.
  - **How it behaves**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00Y' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a Unix socket, a lock file claimed atomically, a queue that handles requests strictly one at a time, a flush of the mirror every 10 seconds when dirty or at once on `flush`, `reload` to read the mirror again, a hash stamp per file so that a flush refuses to overwrite a file that changed on disk, `reload --discard` and `flush --clobber` as the two explicit ways to resolve a conflict, and an exit after 30 idle minutes or on a signal.
- 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-07, steps 6 and 7: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G00Z' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> ingest ordering, and the root block retired: from the archived design (steps 6 and 7).
  
  - **Step 6**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G010' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `ingest --track`; the folder tree rebuilt before any content is parsed or any link resolved, so that a wikilink to a not-yet-attached path does not mint a duplicate holder; folder and file path segments matched the way headings are; a synthetic root block per artifact made invisible to addressing by four patches.
  - **Step 7 (supersedes the four patches)**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G011' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `ArtifactNode` became a `BlockNode` and its own root document, the synthetic root block was removed from storage, and `text` of an artifact and a folder became a copied abstract for both. `FolderNode` stayed on `TreeNode`.
- 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-07, steps 8 and 9: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G012' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> link positions, embedded-node cleanup and tombstone consistency: from the archived design (steps 8 and 9).
  
  - **Step 8**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G013' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `Link` gained `props`; a block keeps one `Link` for each distinct target, carrying one `position` prop for each occurrence, as a start offset relative to the block's own text; the title of a heading is no longer scanned for links. Two bugs: the kind of a nested embedded id was read from its first segment and not its last, and reassigning an embed field detached the old node but never deleted it (now removed recursively).
  - **Step 9**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G014' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> all three tombstone sites clear `children`, `links` and `props`, and removing an artifact tombstones its whole subtree. A wikilink `Link` keeps its id while its target stays; a first version that also required the positions to match was corrected, because a position moves with any earlier edit.
- 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-07, steps 10 and 11: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G015' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> visible tombstones, the garbage collection and the merge of `parent`: from the archived design (steps 10 and 11).
  
  - **Step 10**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G016' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a tombstoned node is printed with a `(tombstoned)` tag, and deleting an embedded node removes the `unfolds` entries that name it; the sweep was made object-bound in the first commit of the performance work (`0a7a226`, below). Its answer to "when is a tombstone ever removed" ("never") is superseded by the next step.
  - **Step 11**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G017' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a tombstoned node is deleted once nothing live reaches it, by a mark and sweep from the live artifacts and folders (a first design that counted any incoming reference was rejected, because a dead cluster would keep itself alive); the stored `parent` field and the private containment predicate, which recorded the same fact twice, were merged into one, a workaround for the `List` limitation of TerminusDB that had outlived its reason; `kg:unlink` was added, the removal counterpart of `kg:link`.
- 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-07, steps 12 and 13: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G018' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the id scheme and the backlinks: from the archived design (steps 12 and 13).
  
  - **Step 12**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G019' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> ids were `/`-separated compound ids under the base `urn:aperas:node:`; they became `:`-separated under `aperas://id/`, and `aperas://tree/` was added as a resolution-time address that is never stored; the test for a full id now requires a valid snowflake after the kind, which closes the hole of a folder named after a kind.
  - **Step 13**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D24PV22G01A' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `kg:backlinks`, the reverse of the forward view of a `Link`, one line for each `Link` whose target is the node.

- 2026-10-11, commit `0a7a226`, "Fix core performance 1": <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8006' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the object-bound `removeDanglingUnfolds`, and the repair of the doubled `#id/` anchor.
  
  - **The sweep**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8007' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `removeDanglingUnfolds` no longer scans every quad once per embedded node removed; it makes one `store.match` on the predicate `unfolds` with the deleted id as object, followed by the deletes. It covers `unfolds`, the only field of a `TreeView` that holds an unfolded id and exists in code. The change rests on [the removal-stall issue](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00CVNX6A3R000) and on [the fix status in the scans issue](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D0X65RB8009).
  - **Equivalence**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8008' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> on a copy of the real store, 59 ids (29 with a hit) gave 43 `unfolds` quads removed and restored with 0 mismatches against the former scan, which stays as a test-only reference in `verifyDanglingUnfolds.ts`.
  - **Measured on scratch copies** (the old code measured first): <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4Z8009' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a removal of 34 embedded nodes went from 21,130 ms with 34 full scans to 12 ms; a removal of a task of 122 nodes, not finished at 600 s (70 scans, 2.6 GB resident), took 24 ms; on a copy of the real store one deleted id cost 512.8 ms before and 0.015 ms after.
  - **Live**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG000' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> after the user committed and restarted the service on 2026-10-11, one `aperas remove --flush` of a finished task of 164 live blocks and 101 embedded `Link` nodes took 3.7 s and exited 0 with no stall, the other tasks of the thread were intact, and `aperas check-links` scanned 6,549 live blocks with 0 discrepancies. The old service had stalled for 5.4 and 12 minutes on removals of 42 and 31 blocks. One removal is one sample. [The removal-stall issue](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00CVNX6A3R000) is recorded resolved for the cause it names, the scan per embedded node.
  - **Not shown**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the other suspected causes of the stall (the link-integrity sweeps, the tombstone hard-deletes at shutdown and startup, a slowdown with the life of the process) were not separated from the scans by any run.
  - **The doubled anchor**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the same commit carries the repair of the doubled `#id/` anchor that the preview of `aperas tree --view` wrote into new text when a reader copied a link from it. The preview renderer skips an href that already contains `#id/`; the writer refuses a repeated `#id/` and a direct-id link whose id names no node, before its first mutation; `aperas check-links` lists a `Link` whose target names no node; an offline sweep of the mirror repaired the five stored targets. It rests on [the doubled-anchor issue](../issues/linking.md#id/BlockNode:00D1A28XNG001) and [its preview-renderer entry](../issues/treeview.md#id/BlockNode:00D1BB9VE8001). On the new code `aperas check-links` was clean over 5,674 live blocks at the repair and over 6,687 live blocks in the restarted service.
- 2026-10-12, commit `d794060`, "Fix core performance 2": <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG003' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> `free()` on the Oxigraph objects, at the root. [The `free()` item of the scans issue](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D1DP5GG0001) holds the survey map the change followed and [its Landed entry](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D216W8Z0001) the details below.
  
  - **The change**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG004' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> ten files, two of them new, `storeAccess.ts` in `core` (250 lines) and its test `store/storeAccess.test.ts`, named in the `commit` suite. Every read returns plain strings, numbers, booleans or rows; each quad is freed after its callback, each argument term in a `finally`, a deleted quad only after its delete, a quad added with its terms after the add; no shared constant is freed; `predIri` is cached per field name. The whole-store `free()` at reload, the plain JavaScript engine and new `TreeView` fields stayed out, as the user's plan says.
  - **Checked by the orchestrator**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG005' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the diff scope and `storeAccess.ts` read in full; `npm run verify -- --suite essential` 27 of 27 in 3.2 s; the `commit` suite 61 of 61 in 19.5 s; `tsc --noEmit` on `core` and `cli` printed nothing. The `demand` and legacy suites were not run.
  - **Measured on a copy of the mirror** (the Coder; production code, 59,275 quads, 3 samples per case, a fresh process each, `gc()` between calls): eight `allIdsOfKind` calls went from 349 to 634 MB (+40 MB a call) to flat at about 265 MB, and from 0.7 to 1.7 s a call to 0.14 to 0.23 s; a dehydrate of both mirrors from 33 to 38 s to 2.5 to 3.8 s, with resident size from about 770 to about 350 MB; a rehydrate from 3.3 to 3.5 s to 2.0 to 2.9 s, with resident size from about 300 to about 258 MB. The dehydrated directories were byte-identical before and after in all three repetitions.
  - **Live check** (2026-10-12, one sample each): <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG007' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the service restarted onto the patch answered 17 s after its process started, at 379 MB resident. `aperas tree --view` took 0.57 s; `aperas check-links` over 6,900 live blocks took 2.7 s twice, clean, with the service at 375 to 379 MB across both scans; the second write into a document took 5.0 s and its projection 2.4 s; resident size went from 379 MB to 424 MB over five commands. In a later run of 12.6 minutes the service went from 390 to 424 MB resident over 63 samples, about 3 MB a minute with no plateau seen, measured from outside the agents; where the time between a write's CPU and its wall time goes, and whether the rise is garbage or retained data, were not decomposed.
  - **Not shown**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG008' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the trend of resident memory over a longer run; the write paths of ingest, `remove` and the tombstone sweeps, which the tests cover and no measurement or live run exercised; the gain split by site; whether releasing the old `Store` at reload is a gain or a hazard. The test-only reads in `verifyDanglingUnfolds.ts` and `verifyLinkRefusals.ts` still read raw quads without `free()`.
- 2026-10-12: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG009' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> Oxigraph patching paused at branch `oxigraph`, the JavaScript ApeironNgn begun on branch `js-apeironngn`. The user's plan of 2026-10-11 was the `free()` helper as the best effort on Oxigraph and no more hacks on it, then a plain JavaScript engine to compare with the `free()` version; [the plan](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D1GC79B8000) is recorded with the scans issue, and the store being only an in-memory reflection of the mirror (the JSON-LD under `Apeiron/`, the source of truth) is why a replacement store does not hold the truth. The `free()` patch was committed as `d794060` on `oxigraph`, which stays as it is; `js-apeironngn` starts from the same commit, and `AperasKG` stays on `main`, shared by both branches. The two engines are to be compared on the same mirror, by their collections as sets and never list by list; Layers 1 to 3 and the pause stay parked on the Oxigraph side. What exists at this date is a survey of the contract the code asks of the store, with no engine code on the branch; no comparison has been measured.
  
  - **The mismatch, analysed** (2026-10-14): <a name='id/BlockNode:00D39EQJY0000' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the cost measured on the Oxigraph store has three causes. Only the first belongs to RDF; the second multiplies it; the third is in the code, and no engine removes it. The rule drawn from it is [Why a node-based engine](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D39EJSZ8001) in the design.
  - **The edge layout**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D39EQJY0001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> Oxigraph holds every field of every document as a quad (`encodeDoc`), a node object holds only the store and its id, and every field read is one bound match and a decode; a lookup by value goes through the predicate-object index, and a list of the ids of a kind is a match over the whole store. The store pays a lookup for each property read, and most of them find nothing: at one flush on a copy of the mirror the Coder's census counted 7,276 reads of `holder`, 7,274 of them empty, and 7,276 reads of `links`, 5,769 of them empty (one flush; [the cost of a write](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D22Z1HJ8001)). One insert made about 170 thousand bound lookups at about 9 microseconds each ([the path index](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D22Z1HJG004); 6 inserts in 3 fresh processes for each of two artifacts), and the timed flush after it made four full scans that materialised 334,075 quads ([the timed flush](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D22Z1HJG006); 3 profiles).
  - **The wasm boundary**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D39EQJY0002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> it is not part of RDF, but it multiplies the cost of the edge layout. Each crossing pays for the wrapper objects of a quad and its terms, their decoding and their release, and the edge layout sets the number of crossings at one for each property read, where a node layout behind the same boundary would cross once for each node. Before `free()`, eight lists of the ids of a kind grew resident memory by 40 MB a call, and a dehydrate of both mirrors took 33 to 38 s; after it, 2.5 to 3.8 s ([the `free()` landing](#id/BlockNode:00D21FP4ZG003); 59,275 quads, 3 samples per case, a fresh process each). A scratch plain JavaScript store of the Coder's with the same triple layout (prototype B) rehydrated a copy of about 60 thousand quads in 174 ms against 2,050 ms for Oxigraph and dehydrated it in 530 ms against 2,500 ms, with about 14 MB of heap against about 107 MB of heap and wasm, and the eight output files byte-identical (one run, 2026-10-12).
  - **The algorithms of a write**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D39EQJY0003' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a small local change recomputes what follows the corpus and not the change, and a faster engine makes each lookup cheaper but not fewer, so these costs are fixed in the code whatever the engine; all are filed in [the cost of a write](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D22Z1HJ8001) and [the full-store scans](../issues/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D0W02KPG001). The path-collision check rebuilds the slug path of every live block for one inserted paragraph, a median of 1,527 to 1,556 ms on a copy of 7,433 live blocks, and recomputes the path of a shared ancestor once for every block below it; two link sweeps of the target artifact parse the text of every block, 831 and 780 ms on a 5,200-line document and 24 and 18 ms on a 37-line one (6 inserts in 3 fresh processes for each artifact); the timed flush within 10 s of a write rewrites all four content files, a median of 1,401 ms (6 flushes); at each flush the thread-scope test climbs every block to its owner, 14,368 path reads in one flush (the census, one flush) and 847 ms of the flush (3 profiles); and every list of the ids of a kind is a scan of the whole store, paid at each call.

- 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-15, [Core — Design](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D24N87SR001) drafted with the user: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3X4321G001' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> from the first draft collected from the archive to the design of a node-based engine, fact-checked against the code and the mirror on 2026-10-12; what is not decided is listed under its [Open points](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D24N87T000J), the work that remains.
  
  - **From RDF triples to a node-based engine**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3X4321G002' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the Oxigraph store held every property as a statement of its own; the design keeps the properties of a node on its record and makes only the relations between nodes pairs, because commands read whole nodes and the vocabulary is closed (see [Why a node-based engine](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D39EJSZ8001)).
  - **The boundary between engine and caller**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3X4321G003' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the engine manages, in memory, the records, the forward and reverse sides of reference and embed fields with their ports, the ordered and partitioning fields, the tombstones and several graphs; the mirror, its format, the load, the flush and the maps for lookups by a literal value are its caller's (see [The boundary](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D3CJFB7G001)).
  - **Pairs and ports**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3X4321G004' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> a field is a fixed relation and a pair is one link of it, held on its subject's forward side and on its value's reverse side; a pair joins an outgoing port of its subject to an incoming port of its object (see [Ports, the two ends of a pair](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D3CJCK10001)).
  - **Ordered and partitioning fields**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3X4321G005' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> an ordered field is a list on its host, with no sibling index, so an insertion or a move is one splice of the list; a partitioning field gives each node at most one value on the climbed side and has no cycle; containment, the field `children`, is both (see [Ordered fields](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D255WVK0001) and [Partitioning fields](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D3CJDGH8001)).
  - **Lazy reverse with ports**: <a name='id/BlockNode:00D3X4321G006' class='aperas-anchor aperas-id'></a> the reverse of a field is built the first time the field is asked in reverse and kept up to date by every write afterwards, held on the records of the objects; a bucket over its field's cap is split into ports along a partitioning field (see [Reverse, for each field, built on first use](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D24N87SR017) and [Ports](../design/core.md#id/BlockNode:00D2T5VG08001)).
